# Photography Camera Settings (9 Years of Camera Knowledge)

## 1. Core Concept: Exposure
- **Exposure** = total amount of light passing through the **Aperture** (lens) → **Shutter** → hitting the **Sensor / Film (ISO)**.
- **3 Types of Exposure:**
  - **Incorrect Exposure:** Too dark or too bright.
  - **Correct Exposure:** Brightness, shadows, and highlights look fine (what Auto mode gives), but depth of field and motion are ignored.
  - **Creatively Correct Exposure:** You deliberately choose **Aperture**, **Shutter Speed**, and **ISO** to control sharpness, background blur, and motion.

### 4 Questions Before Every Shot
1. What **ISO** (or film) should I use?
2. How much **Depth of Field** (background blur) do I want?
3. Is there **Motion** in the frame (or camera shake)?
4. How do I want that motion to look (**frozen** or **blurred/streaked**)?

## 2. The Exposure Triangle & Stops
- **Aperture, Shutter Speed, and ISO** are connected: changing one changes brightness, so you must compensate with one or both of the others.
- **Stop** = doubling or halving the light:
  - **+1 stop** = double the light
  - **-1 stop** = half the light
- **How stops work on each setting:**
  - **Shutter Speed:** Halving/doubling time = 1 stop (e.g., `1/60s` → `1/125s` = `-1 stop`; `1/60s` → `1/30s` = `+1 stop`).
  - **ISO:** Halving/doubling number = 1 stop (e.g., `100` → `200` → `400` → `800`).
  - **Aperture:** Film lenses mark full stops directly on the ring; digital dials usually move in **1/3 stop** per click.

## 3. Aperture (F-Stop) — Light & Depth of Field
- **What it is:** The size of your lens's entrance pupil (opening).
- **F-Stop Formula:** `F` (focal length) `/` `stop number` (e.g., on a `50mm` lens, `F/2.8` = `17.85mm` opening; `F/22` = `2.27mm` opening).
- **Reversed Number Rule:**
  - **Smaller F-number (`F/1.4`, `F/2.8`)** = larger opening = **more light** + **shallow depth of field** (blurry background, great for portraits).
  - **Larger F-number (`F/11`, `F/22`)** = smaller opening = **less light** + **deep depth of field** (more foreground & background in focus).
- **Landscape Sweet Spot: `F/8` to `F/11`** — Sharpens foreground and background without closing down too far (at `F/22`, even dust on the front of the lens can come into focus).
- **Extra Blur Tips:**
  - Depth of field gets shallower when you **zoom in more** and have **more distance** between your subject and the background.
  - When everything is far away with no foreground (e.g., zooming into distant mountains), `F/2.8` and `F/22` look nearly identical.

## 4. ISO — Digital Gain vs. Film Sensitivity
- **Digital:** ISO is **gain** (electrical boost through the sensor). Higher gain = brighter image, **more digital noise** (usually unappealing). Can be changed anytime.
- **Film:** Fixed by the roll's **box speed** (e.g., `Velvia 50`, `Ektar 100`, `ColorPlus 200`, `Portra 400`, `Portra 800`). Higher ISO film = more **film grain**.
- **Base ISO:** Usually the lowest native ISO (e.g., `125`), where the sensor performs cleanest. **Stay at Base ISO whenever light allows.**
- **High ISO Trade-off:** In low light without a tripod, pushing ISO (even up to `12,800`) is worth the grain if it lets you keep a fast enough shutter speed for a sharp handheld shot.

## 5. Shutter Speed — Light & Motion
- **What it is:** How long the shutter stays open when you press the shutter button.
- **Fast Shutter (`1/250s`, `1/500s`, `1/8000s`):** Less light, **freezes motion** (crisp water droplets, running subject).
- **Slow Shutter (`1/30s`, `1s–10s`, `30s`):** More light, **blurs/streaks motion** (soft waterfalls, car light trails).
  - *Waterfall example:* `1s` keeps more water texture/definition; `10s` makes the water extra soft and smooth.
- **Handheld Rule (avoid camera shake):**
  - **`1/60s` or faster** = generally safe handheld.
  - **`1/30s` or slower** = likely to introduce blur from hand shake.
- **Long Exposure / Tripod Tip:**
  - Most cameras max out natively at **30 seconds**.
  - Put the camera on a **tripod**, use **Base ISO**, and set a **2-second self-timer** so pressing the shutter button doesn't shake the camera.

## 6. Neutral Density (ND / VND) Filters
- **What it is:** Dark glass placed in front of the lens to cut down light (sunglasses for the lens).
- **Variable ND (VND):** Two polarizing filters stacked together—rotate to adjust how much light is blocked.
- **When to use it:** In bright daylight, when you want a **slow shutter speed** (`1s–10s` for soft water) while keeping **Base ISO** and your chosen **Aperture** (`F/11`).

## 7. Priority Modes (Between Auto & Manual)
Great for getting the same creative results with less work, and learning how the camera compensates:

- **Aperture Priority (`A` / `Av`):**
  - **You set:** Aperture (depth of field) + Base ISO (or **Auto ISO** in low light).
  - **Camera sets:** Shutter Speed.
  - **Use when:** Depth of field matters most and motion isn't a problem (landscapes, portraits, still scenes).
- **Shutter Priority (`S` / `Tv`):**
  - **You set:** Shutter Speed (e.g., `1/500s`–`1/8000s` to freeze action).
  - **Camera sets:** Aperture and ISO.
  - **Use when:** Motion is the main variable (sports, running, fast action).
- **When to switch to Full Manual (`M`):**
  - When **both** Depth of Field and Motion must be controlled together (e.g., daylight long-exposure waterfalls, handheld low-light shots needing `F/11` + `1/60s`, or `30s` night light trails on a tripod).
